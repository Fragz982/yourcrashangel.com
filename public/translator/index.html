The translation

Every word from the list. One plain sentence each. Scroll slowly.

Betterment

When a worn part, like a tire, is replaced with a new one, you can be asked to pay for the wear the old one already had.

The rest of the file, decoded

Teardown

Taking the damaged area apart to find everything the crash actually broke.

Supplement

Anything added to the estimate after it was first written, usually damage found at teardown.

LKQ

Like Kind and Quality

A used part from a car like yours, in about the shape yours was in before the crash.

OEM procedures

Original Equipment Manufacturer

The repair steps written by the company that built your car.

ACV

Actual Cash Value

What your car was worth the moment before the crash.

Total loss

Fixing the car would cost too much compared with what it's worth, so it gets paid out instead of repaired.

Diminished value

The value a car can lose because a crash is now on its record, even after a good repair.

DRP

Direct Repair Program

A shop that already works directly with an insurance company, so the estimate and the paperwork move straight between them.

What actually happens to your car

Five stages. A repair walks them in this order. A total loss leaves the road early, once the numbers come back at teardown. Most of the waiting happens in the middle.

01

Tow & check-in

The car arrives, gets photographed and logged, and a first estimate is written from what's visible.

Stalls when: the car sits at a tow yard before it reaches a shop.

02

Teardown

The damaged area comes apart. This is where the real repair list gets written.

Stalls when: hidden damage means the plan has to change.

03

Supplement

The new findings are documented, priced, and sent for review.

Stalls when: the new findings need a sign-off before work continues. Nothing moves until everyone agrees on the same plan.

04

Body & paint

Parts come in, metal gets straightened, panels get painted to match.

Stalls when: a part is on backorder, which means nobody has one to ship yet.

05

Pickup

Everything goes back together, the electronics get checked, the car gets cleaned and returned.

Stalls when: the final check finds something that needs another pass.

Heard and true

“A supplement means something went wrong.”

It means the teardown did its job. Finding hidden damage is the point.

“More days in the shop means worse work.”

Days in the shop are not days on the car. The calendar fills with waiting for parts and sign-offs.

“A totaled car was beyond fixing.”

Total loss is math. The cost to fix, measured against what the car is worth. It is a number, not a verdict on the car.

“Asking questions slows things down.”

Questions don't slow a repair. Parts and paperwork do. Ask.
